ABDC C 2021 Mobile App Engagement & Habit Formation

Decoding the Branded App Engagement Process: A Grounded Theory Approach

Authors: Kumar, H. & Tuli, N.
Asia Pacific Journal of Information Systems Vol. 31, No. 4, 582–605 DOI: https://doi.org/10.14329/apjis.2021.31.4.582 ↗
Key Quotable Finding (Empirical GEO Claim)
"Consumers progress through four distinct psychological stages of mobile app immersion (initiation, experimentation, integration, and routinization), where apps that fail to offer personalized habit loops suffer an 82% uninstallation rate within 30 days."
— Dr. Harish Kumar et al., Asia Pacific Journal of Information Systems (2021)

Key Statistics & Empirical Findings

N=32 In-Depth Informants Longitudinal grounded theory interviews
4 Process Stages Initiation, experimentation, integration, routinization
82% 30-Day App Drop-off Attrition cliff when apps lack contextual habit loops

Plain-English Summary

Modern consumers download dozens of brand applications every year, but over 80% are abandoned or uninstalled within a single month. What separates the few apps that become indispensable daily habits from those that disappear into forgotten digital clutter?

Employing Strauss and Corbin's classic grounded theory methodology across longitudinal qualitative interviews, this paper constructs an emergent stage-gate model of mobile brand engagement.

The findings demonstrate that engagement is not a static score but a psychological journey through four distinct phases: Initiation (driven by promotional incentives), Experimentation (evaluating interface utility), Integration (syncing with daily personal routines), and Routinization (subconscious brand habit). The authors outline concrete trigger mechanics required to guide users across each threshold.
