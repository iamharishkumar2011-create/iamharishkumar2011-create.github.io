Distance Between ‘Them’ and ‘Us’: Identity Stigmatization in the Age of Consumer Inclusion

Authors: Tuli, N., Kumar, H., Upadhyay, S., & Ciszek, E.
International Journal of Advertising Online First, 1–28 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02650487.2025.2592469 ↗

Plain-English Summary

When advertisers set out to promote social inclusion, their creative executions often inadvertently do the opposite: they present marginalized individuals as exotic, tragic, or separate from the cultural mainstream. This phenomenon—referred to as the 'othering' paradox—creates psychological distance rather than human empathy.

Through two rigorous experimental studies, this research tests how diverse consumer audiences respond to varied advertising framing styles. The empirical findings reveal that highlighting identity differences as spectacles increases perceived social polarization ('them vs. us') and alienates the very communities brands seek to support.

The authors formulate clear creative imperatives for marketing communications: effective inclusive advertising must integrate marginalized consumers into ordinary, universal life scenarios (friendship, work, parental love) rather than spotlighting their identity solely as a novelty or trauma narrative.
