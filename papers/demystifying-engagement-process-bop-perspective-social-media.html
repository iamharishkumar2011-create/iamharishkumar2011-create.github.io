Plain-English Summary

The vast majority of consumer research into social media engagement is conducted on affluent university students or middle-class western consumers with high-speed laptops and disposable income. But how do the four billion people living at the Bottom-of-the-Pyramid (BoP) experience social digital channels?

This field study travels into semi-urban communities in northern India to examine how low-income consumers, artisans, and micro-entrepreneurs use low-cost mobile smartphones and social media networks like WhatsApp, YouTube, and Instagram.

The research reveals that for BoP consumers, engagement is fundamentally transactional and developmental: social media serves as a decentralized business catalog, vocational classroom, and community safety net. By providing accessible digital visibility, social platforms empower economically vulnerable individuals to bypass predatory middlemen.
