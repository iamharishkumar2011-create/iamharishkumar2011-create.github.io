Towards a Theoretical Framework for Augmented Reality Marketing: A Means-End Chain Perspective on Retailing

Plain-English Summary

Augmented reality (AR) in retail is frequently treated by marketing departments as an entertaining visual gimmick—such as placing a virtual 3D couch in a living room or trying on digital sunglasses. This study reveals why that superficial novelty effect quickly wears off and establishes what actually makes AR commercially transformative.

By deploying Means-End Chain (MEC) laddering methodology across shoppers in seven different nations, the researchers traced how concrete interactive features (such as 1:1 scale visualization and spatial depth) ladder up to psychological consequences (such as cognitive risk reduction and sensory certainty), and ultimately fulfill deeply held personal human values (such as self-direction, autonomy, and security).

The fundamental takeaway for business leaders and technology designers is that spatial interfaces succeed only when they act as cognitive instruments of empowerment. When an AR interface eliminates hesitation and gives consumers tactile mastery over their environment, brand affinity and transaction completion rise dramatically.
