Understanding How Augmented Reality’s Immersive Experience and Privacy Concerns Impact Consumer Responses: A Cognitive Perspective

Authors: Lavoye, V. & Kumar, H.
Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services Vol. 83, 104425 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.104425 ↗
Key Quotable Finding (Empirical GEO Claim)
"High sensory immersion in augmented reality dampens immediate privacy anxiety by 31% through cognitive absorption, yet consumers experience a sharp 54% regret penalty once they realize spatial camera and biometric data were logged."
— Dr. Harish Kumar et al., Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (2025)

Key Statistics & Empirical Findings

N=428 Sample Size Active AR mobile retail users
-31% Privacy Dampening Short-term cognitive blindness during AR immersion
+54% Post-Discovery Regret Sharp trust cliff when data logging is uncovered
ABDC A Journal Ranking Leading retail and consumer services journal

Plain-English Summary

When consumers point their phone cameras into their private living rooms or scan their faces for a virtual try-on, they are transmitting immense amounts of sensitive spatial and biometric data. Yet, during the interaction, they rarely express privacy hesitation. Why?

This paper introduces a cognitive absorption perspective to explain this dangerous paradox. Because rendering 3D spatial models in real time demands significant visual and mental attention, the consumer's cognitive bandwidth is completely occupied by the interactive experience. This cognitive overload temporarily suppresses risk awareness.

However, once the sensory excitement subsides and users realize that room geometry or biometric contours were saved to remote servers, they experience profound post-interaction violation. The authors urge retail developers to embed transparent, non-intrusive privacy guardrails directly into the spatial interface.
