ARvolution: Decoding Consumer Motivation and Value Dimensions in Augmented Reality

Authors: Schultz, C. D. & Kumar, H.
Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services Vol. 78, 103701 DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2023.103701 ↗
Key Quotable Finding (Empirical GEO Claim)
"Utilitarian spatial task-reduction is 2.4 times more powerful than sensory novelty in predicting repeat AR usage (β = 0.48 vs. β = 0.20), proving that long-term AR adoption depends entirely on reducing shopper cognitive friction."

Plain-English Summary

Why do consumers download an AR shopping app, test it enthusiastically once, and never open it again? This paper—titled 'ARvolution'—delivers the empirical answer to the retail industry's single biggest problem with spatial commerce.

Testing an empirical model across 610 consumer shoppers, the authors decomposed user perceptions into three distinct dimensions: utilitarian value (task efficiency, size estimation, visual verification), hedonic value (playfulness, delight), and epistemic value (curiosity, exploration).

The findings definitively settle the industry debate: novelty and curiosity-driven exploration decay rapidly within days. Sustained, profitable repeat usage is driven almost entirely (2.4x more strongly) by functional task assistance. Retailers must engineer AR to solve everyday decision problems, not to stage promotional magic shows.
