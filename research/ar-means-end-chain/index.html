Why augmented reality sells: a means-end framework

In One Line AR works in marketing because its features deliver concrete benefits that serve consumers' deeper personal values.
Information & Management (2024) DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.im.2023.103910 ↗
🔓 Open Access Repository Version:
Free to Read (University of Bamberg repository) ↗
Key Finding (Quotable Scientific Claim)
"Based on 36 laddering interviews and two surveys (N = 411), the study organises AR's consumer value into two frameworks: SEAD (sensory, efficiency, assessment, discovery) for benefits and SALES (status, achievement, lifestyle, economy, safety) for values. This is one of the key theory development papers in AR marketing."
— Dr. Harish Kumar, Information & Management (2024)

Plain-English Summary

Marketers know augmented reality (AR) often outperforms other formats, but nobody had explained why from the consumer's side. Using means-end chain theory, we traced specific AR features such as contextualization, interactivity and portability to benefits such as inspiration, better decisions, time savings and lower risk. Those benefits in turn serve personal goals such as self-confidence, self-expression and less purchase regret.

For Managers & Industry Leaders

Strategic Implication: Design AR experiences not just for visual novelty, but as functional pathways that fulfill consumers' underlying human goals of autonomy, lifestyle enhancement, and confidence.
